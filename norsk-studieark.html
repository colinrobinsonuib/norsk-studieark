Norsk Studieark — Norwegian Quick Reference

1. Konjunksjoner — Conjunctions

Sideordnende — Coordinating

og — and
Jeg liker kaffe og te. — I like coffee and tea.
men — but
Han er ung, men veldig smart. — He is young, but very smart.
eller — or
Vil du ha kaffe eller te? — Do you want coffee or tea?
for — for, because — literary/formal "because"; does NOT invert word order
Jeg gikk hjem, for jeg var trøtt. — I went home, for I was tired.
så — so, then
Det regnet, så vi ble hjemme. — It rained, so we stayed home.
samt — as well as, and (formal) — more formal than "og"; used in lists and official writing
Mat samt drikke er inkludert. — Food as well as drinks are included.
både … og — both … and
Både barn og voksne likte filmen. — Both children and adults liked the film.
enten … eller — either … or
Enten kommer du nå, eller så drar vi. — Either you come now, or we leave.
verken … eller — neither … nor
Han liker verken fisk eller kjøtt. — He likes neither fish nor meat.

Underordnende — Subordinating

⚠ Subordinating conjunctions cause leddsetning (sub-clause) word order: subject + ikke + verb.
fordi — because
Jeg ble hjemme fordi jeg var syk. — I stayed home because I was sick.
at — that — introduces a noun clause (see also Section 4)
Jeg tror at det stemmer. — I think that it's correct.
hvis / dersom — if — "dersom" is more formal; both mean conditional "if"
Hvis det regner, tar jeg paraply. — If it rains, I'll take an umbrella.
selv om — even though, although
Han gikk ut selv om det regnet. — He went out even though it was raining.
mens — while, whereas
Hun leste mens han lagde mat. — She read while he cooked.
siden — since (causal / temporal)
Siden du er her, kan du hjelpe. — Since you're here, you can help.
slik at — so that
Snakk sakte slik at alle forstår. — Speak slowly so that everyone understands.
uten at — without (+ clause)
Han gikk uten at noen la merke til det. — He left without anyone noticing.

2. Preposisjoner — Prepositions

Sted — Location

i — in, inside
Boka er i sekken. — The book is in the bag.
Hun bor i Oslo. — She lives in Oslo.
på — on, at — "on" a surface; "at" a place (på jobb, på skolen)
Koppen er på bordet. — The cup is on the table.
Jeg er på jobb. — I'm at work.
ved — by, near, at
Huset ligger ved sjøen. — The house is by the sea.
bak — behind
Katten sitter bak sofaen. — The cat is sitting behind the sofa.
foran — in front of
Bilen er foran huset. — The car is in front of the house.
mellom — between, among
Banken er mellom butikken og kafeen. — The bank is between the shop and the café.
over — over, above
Lampa henger over bordet. — The lamp hangs over the table.
under — under, below
Hunden ligger under bordet. — The dog is under the table.
blant — among, amongst
Hun er populær blant venner. — She is popular among friends.
innenfor — within, inside (a boundary) — emphasizes being within limits or boundaries
Innenfor budsjettet. — Within the budget.
Bli innenfor! — Stay inside!
utenfor — outside (a boundary)
De ventet utenfor døra. — They waited outside the door.

Retning — Direction / Particles

til — to, for
Jeg drar til Bergen. — I'm going to Bergen.
En gave til deg. — A gift for you.
fra — from
Hun er fra Norge. — She is from Norway.
ut — out
Gå ut! — Go out!
inn — in (direction)
Kom inn! — Come in!
opp — up
Gå opp trappen. — Go up the stairs.
ned — down
Han gikk ned til sjøen. — He walked down to the sea.
gjennom — through
Vi kjørte gjennom tunnelen. — We drove through the tunnel.
mot — towards, against
Han gikk mot døra. — He walked towards the door.
De spilte mot hverandre. — They played against each other.
forbi — past, by
Vi kjørte forbi huset. — We drove past the house.

Tid — Time

etter — after
Etter middag. — After dinner.
før — before
Før klokka ti. — Before ten o'clock.
under — during
Under krigen. — During the war.
i — in, for (duration)
I to timer. — For two hours.
I juni. — In June.
på — on (days); in (time expressions)
På mandag. — On Monday.
På morgenen. — In the morning.
om — in (future time)
Om to timer. — In two hours. (from now)
siden — ago, since
To dager siden. — Two days ago.

Forhold — Relationship

for — for (purpose/benefit)
Det er bra for deg. — It's good for you.
med — with
Jeg bor med familien min. — I live with my family.
av — of, off, by
En av oss. — One of us.
Boka er skrevet av Ibsen. — The book is written by Ibsen.
uten — without
Kaffe uten melk. — Coffee without milk.
om — about
En bok om Norge. — A book about Norway.

3. Pronomen — Pronouns

Personlige pronomen — Personal Pronouns

English Subject Object Possessive (en / ei / et / fl)
I / me / my jeg meg min / mi / mitt / mine
you / your du deg din / di / ditt / dine
he / him / his han ham hans
she / her hun henne hennes
it (en-word) den den dens (rare)
it (et-word) det det dets (rare)
we / us / our vi oss vår / vår / vårt / våre
you (pl) / your dere dere deres
they / them / their de dem deres
💡 Possessives agree with the noun they modify, not the owner.
min bok (en-word) · mi bok (feminine) · mitt hus (et-word) · mine bøker (plural)

Refleksiv: seg / sin / si / sitt / sine

The reflexive refers back to the 3rd-person subject of the same clause. Used for he/she/it/they when referring to their own things.
Form Agrees with Example
seg object (himself/herself/themselves) Han vasker seg. — He washes himself.
sin en-word noun Han leser boken sin. — He reads his (own) book.
si ei-word noun (feminine) Hun leser boka si. — She reads her (own) book.
sitt et-word noun Hun liker huset sitt. — She likes her (own) house.
sine plural noun De elsker barna sine. — They love their (own) children.
✓ sin = own
Han vasker bilen sin.
He washes his own car.
✗ hans = someone else's
Han vasker bilen hans.
He washes his (another man's) car.
⚠ seg/sin is ONLY for 3rd person. For "I/you/we" use meg/deg/oss and min/mi/mitt/mine, din/di/ditt/dine, vår/vårt/våre.
Jeg vasker bilen min. (NOT *sin) — I wash my car.

4. Å si "that" — Ways to Say "That"

at — that (conjunction) — connects a verb of thinking/saying/knowing to a clause
Jeg vet at du har rett. — I know that you are right.
Hun sa at hun skulle komme. — She said that she would come.
Jeg tror at det stemmer. — I think that it's correct.
💡 "at" can often be dropped in casual speech: "Jeg tror det stemmer."
som — that / which / who (relative pronoun) — introduces a relative clause describing a noun
Mannen som bor her. — The man who lives here.
Boka som jeg leser. — The book that I'm reading.
Huset som ligger ved sjøen. — The house that is by the sea.
💡 Norwegian uses "som" for who, which, and that — one word covers all three!
det — that (demonstrative / pronoun) — pointing at something; "that thing"
Det er riktig. — That is correct.
Hva er det? — What is that?
Jeg liker det. — I like that.
den / det … som — the one that — picks out a specific item from a group
Den som jobber hardt, lykkes. — The one who works hard succeeds.
Det som skjedde var trist. — What (that which) happened was sad.
at → after verbs
Jeg vet at …
Jeg tror at …
Hun sa at …
som → after nouns
Mannen som …
Boka som …
Stedet som …

5. "It" og Demonstrativer — "It" & Demonstratives

Den / Det / De / Dem — "It" & "They"

den — it (for en-words)
Hvor er boka? Den er på bordet. — Where is the book? It's on the table.
det — it (for et-words); also dummy subject
Huset er fint. Det er stort. — The house is nice. It is big.
Det regner. — It's raining. (dummy subject — no real "it")
Det er mange folk her. — There are many people here. (existential "det")
de — they (subject)
De bor i Oslo. — They live in Oslo.
dem — them (object)
Jeg ser dem. — I see them.
💡 Gender rule: den → masculine/feminine nouns (bilen, stolen, boka) · det → neuter nouns (huset, bordet, barnet)

Denne / Dette / Disse — Demonstratives

en-word et-word Plural
this / these denne dette disse
that / those den (der) det (der) de (der)
💡 Plain den/det/de can mean "that/those," but to clearly contrast with "this/these," add der:
den der bilen = that car (over there) · de der folkene = those people
denne — this (en-word)
Denne boka er god. — This book is good.
dette — this (et-word)
Dette huset er gammelt. — This house is old.
Hva er dette? — What is this?
disse — these
Disse bøkene er mine. — These books are mine.

6. Da vs. Når — When

da = single past event
A specific time in the past; happened once.
når = repeated / future / question
Habitual, future, or asking "when?"
da — when (one-time past); then
Da jeg var barn, bodde vi i Bergen. — When I was a child, we lived in Bergen.
Da hun kom hjem, var det mørkt. — When she came home, it was dark.
Jeg spiste, og da gikk jeg. — I ate, and then I left. (da = then)
når — when (repeated / future); whenever
Når det regner, tar jeg paraply. — Whenever it rains, I take an umbrella.
Når jeg blir stor, vil jeg bli lege. — When I grow up, I want to be a doctor.
Når kommer du? — When are you coming? (question)
Quick test: Can you replace "when" with "whenever"? → Use når.
Is it a specific past moment? → Use da.
Asking a question with "when"? → Always når.
Huskeregel: Den gang da, hver gang når. — That time: da. Every time: når.

7. Flerbruksord — Words with Multiple Meanings

som
① that/which/who (relative): Gutten som leker. — The boy who plays.
② as, like (comparison): Hvit som snø. — White as snow.
③ like (resemblance): Det ser ut som regn. — It looks like rain.
④ like (comparison of people/things): Han er ikke som broren sin. — He is not like his brother.
⑤ as (role): Hun jobber som lege. — She works as a doctor.
om
① about: En bok om Norge. — A book about Norway.
② if (indirect question): Jeg lurer på om det stemmer. — I wonder if it's true.
③ in (future time): Om to timer. — In two hours.
④ in (time of day): Vi pleier å gå tur om kvelden. — We usually go for a walk in the evening.
av
① of: En av oss. — One of us.
② off: Ta av deg skoene. — Take off your shoes.
③ of/from (material): Laget av tre. — Made of wood.
at
① that (conjunction): Jeg vet at du kan. — I know that you can.
Only one meaning — but appears everywhere! See Section 4.
jo — untranslatable emphasis particle; adds "as you know / obviously / actually"
Du vet jo det. — You know that (obviously / don't you?).
Det er jo sant! — It IS true! (reinforcing)
Jo, det stemmer. — Yes (contradicting a negative). ("jo" = yes to a negative question)
vel
① presumably, I suppose (particle): Det er vel sant. — That's probably/presumably true.
② well (filler): Vel, det er slik det er. — Well, that's how it is.
nok
① enough: Vi har nok mat. — We have enough food.
② probably (particle): Det er nok sant. — That's probably true.
da (beyond Section 6)
① when (past): Da jeg var ung… — When I was young…
② then: Hva gjør vi da? — What do we do then?
③ emphasis: Kom da! — Come on! / Det var da rart. — That was strange (indeed).
jo … jo / dess … dess — the more … the more
Jo mer du øver, jo bedre blir du. — The more you practice, the better you get.
Dess større, dess bedre. — The bigger, the better.
noen / noe — some, any, someone, something
Er det noen her? — Is anyone here? (noen = someone/anyone)
Har du noe vann? — Do you have some/any water? (noe = something/any, uncountable)
Noen bøker. — Some books. (noen + plural = some)

8. Spørreord — Question Words

Grunnleggende — Basic Question Words

hva — what
Hva heter du? — What is your name?
Hva skjedde? — What happened?
hvem — who
Hvem er det? — Who is that?
Hvem snakker du med? — Who are you talking to?
hvor — where
Hvor bor du? — Where do you live?
Hvor er toalettet? — Where is the toilet?
hvorfor — why
Hvorfor gråter du? — Why are you crying?
Hvorfor ikke? — Why not?
når — when (question) — see also Section 6 for da vs når
Når begynner filmen? — When does the film start?
hvilken / hvilket / hvilke — which — agrees with noun gender: hvilken (en) · hvilket (et) · hvilke (pl)
Hvilken bok liker du best? — Which book do you like best?
Hvilket språk snakker du? — Which language do you speak?
Hvilke farger liker du? — Which colors do you like?

Hvordan vs. Hvor — Ways to Say "How"

⚠ Norwegian splits English "how" into two words with different roles.
hvordan — how (in what way / method) — asks about manner, process, or method
Hvordan har du det? — How are you?
Hvordan lager du pizza? — How do you make pizza?
Hvordan kom du hit? — How did you get here?
Hvordan fungerer det? — How does it work?
hvor — how (+ adjective/adverb = degree) — asks about degree, amount, quantity, distance, speed, etc.
Hvor gammel er du? — How old are you?
Hvor langt er det? — How far is it?
Hvor lenge har du bodd her? — How long have you lived here?
Hvor mye koster det? — How much does it cost?
Hvor mange barn har du? — How many children do you have?
Hvor fort kjørte du? — How fast were you driving?
hvordan = "in what way?"
Hvordan lærte du norsk?
How did you learn Norwegian?
→ answer describes a method
hvor + adj = "to what degree?"
Hvor vanskelig er norsk?
How difficult is Norwegian?
→ answer describes a degree
💡 Rule of thumb: If "how" asks about degree, amount, or quantity → hvor (hvor mye, hvor mange, hvor langt…).
If "how" asks about method or manner → hvordan.
"Hvor" literally means "where" — think of "where on the scale?" to remember.
